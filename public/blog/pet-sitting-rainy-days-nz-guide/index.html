Rainy Day Pet Sitting in New Zealand: Keeping Pets Active and Happy

Published 28 August 2026 · 9 min read · ZiggySitters NZ

A wet forecast does not have to mean a bored dog, a restless cat or a missed pet sitting visit. With the right mix of safe outdoor time, indoor enrichment and drying routines, pets can stay comfortable through New Zealand’s changeable weather.

From Auckland downpours to Wellington wind and long West Coast showers, rain is part of life in Aotearoa. When owners are away, a pet sitter in New Zealand needs more than a backup walk: they need a weather-aware plan that protects paws, prevents boredom and keeps the home dry.

Make a weather plan before the booking starts

Ask the owner what their pet normally does in wet weather. Some dogs are happy in a raincoat; others refuse to leave the porch. Some cats become active indoors; others want a covered outdoor area. Agree on a minimum routine rather than promising a particular route in every forecast.

  • Check the forecast and daylight before each visit.
  • Avoid exposed beaches, river edges and slippery tracks during severe weather.
  • Confirm where towels, coats, paw balm, enrichment toys and cleaning products are stored.
  • Know the household’s storm plan, including power cuts and emergency contacts.
  • Message the owner if rain changes the agreed activity, with a photo of the alternative enrichment.
Safety first: Never turn a pet sitting visit into an adventure during lightning, flooding, dangerous wind or rapidly rising water. A shorter, calm routine is better than a risky walk.

Safe outdoor exercise for wet days

Dogs still benefit from movement and sniffing, even when the outing is brief. Choose familiar paths with good drainage and visibility. Keep the lead secure near roads, drains, streams and livestock. Wet grass can conceal holes, while muddy surfaces can become slippery for older dogs.

Break a long walk into two shorter outings if that suits the dog. A ten-minute sniff around a safe block can be more mentally satisfying than a hurried kilometre. Carry waste bags, use reflective gear in low light and do not assume a waterproof coat replaces supervision: some dogs overheat indoors after returning in heavy layers.

The post-walk drying routine

Wet fur left against skin can cause discomfort and matting, especially around ears, armpits, collars and paws. On return, towel-dry the dog calmly, check between the toes for grit or small stones, and remove a wet coat so the skin can breathe. Long-haired dogs may need gentle brushing once the coat is partly dry.

Do not use a hot hairdryer without the owner’s instructions. If the pet is shivering, unusually quiet, limping or repeatedly licking a paw, contact the owner and follow their veterinary plan. Wash muddy paws before the dog licks them, particularly after areas treated with unknown chemicals or fertiliser.

Indoor enrichment for dogs

When weather rules out a proper walk, replace distance with purposeful activity. Scatter part of the dog’s normal meal around a safe room for supervised sniffing. Use a stuffed food toy, practise short training sessions, or create a simple “find it” game with treats approved by the owner.

  • Five minutes of sit, touch, stay and recall practice.
  • Food puzzles that match the dog’s experience and cannot be chewed apart.
  • Tug or fetch in a clear hallway, with non-slip flooring and no fragile furniture nearby.
  • Calm brushing and handling practice for dogs who enjoy it.
  • Rotating familiar toys instead of leaving every toy out at once.

Do not introduce a new diet, high-value chew or intense game without permission. Rainy-day care should enrich the pet without creating an upset stomach, resource guarding or an injury.

Indoor enrichment for cats

Cats often appreciate predictable, low-pressure interaction. Offer a wand toy, cardboard box, paper ball or treat search, then allow the cat to stop. Place a perch near a window if the room is secure, and rotate toys so they remain interesting. Keep string, ribbon and small detachable parts supervised and put away afterward.

Rain can make windows and doors more tempting escape points. Keep flyscreens secure, check that the cat flap is operating correctly and never leave a door propped open for convenience. If the owner uses indoor-only rules, follow them even if the weather briefly clears.

Small pets and birds need weather protection too

Rabbits, guinea pigs and birds can be affected by cold draughts, damp bedding and sudden temperature changes. Follow the owner’s instructions for hutch covers and ventilation; do not seal an enclosure completely because fresh air still matters. Replace wet bedding, check water bottles and watch for a change in appetite or droppings.

For birds, keep cages away from draughts and do not move them into a kitchen or bathroom unless the owner has specifically instructed it. For reptiles, follow the established heating and humidity setup rather than improvising based on the weather outside.

How owners can brief a rainy-day pet sitter

Leave a “bad weather” page with the pet’s indoor games, exercise minimum, coat size, towel location, paw-cleaning instructions and situations that require a call. Include photos of any ramps, gates or slippery areas that need attention. Tell the sitter whether muddy towels can go in the washing machine and where wet equipment should dry.

Most importantly, define what counts as an emergency: flooding near the property, a fallen tree, a power outage affecting medical equipment, or a pet showing signs of illness. A clear plan prevents hesitation when conditions change.

Choosing a weather-aware pet sitter in NZ

At a meet-and-greet, ask how the sitter handles a forecast that cancels the normal walk. Look for practical answers: safe alternatives, communication, drying and supervision. A dependable sitter will respect the pet’s tolerance rather than forcing an outdoor activity for the sake of a checklist.

Agree on updates after wet visits. A quick note such as “short sniff walk, paws dried, puzzle feeder completed, settled on the bed” tells you the pet’s needs were met even when the weather was miserable.

Rainy-day checklist

  • Check conditions before leaving.
  • Choose a safe, visible route or indoor alternative.
  • Keep pets away from floodwater and unstable trees.
  • Dry paws, coat and ears after outdoor time.
  • Replace the missed exercise with supervised enrichment.
  • Report unusual behaviour, injury or home damage promptly.
