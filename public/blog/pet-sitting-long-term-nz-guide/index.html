Long-Term Pet Sitting in New Zealand: A Practical Planning Guide

Published 28 August 2026 · 10 min read · ZiggySitters NZ

A weekend away is easy to organise. Three weeks, two months, or an overseas work placement is different. Long-term pet sitting in New Zealand works best when the arrangement is treated as a care plan: clear routines, a named backup, written permissions, and regular check-ins.

Leaving pets for an extended period can feel uncomfortable even when the trip is important. Dogs may need familiar walks; cats often cope better at home than during a move; rabbits, birds and other small animals can require precise daily care. The right long-term pet sitter NZ arrangement keeps those needs consistent while giving you one accountable person to contact.

When long-term pet sitting is the right choice

In-home care is worth considering for an extended absence when your pet is settled in its own environment, has a predictable routine, or becomes stressed by boarding. It can also suit owners travelling for work, taking a long New Zealand road trip, recovering in hospital, or spending an extended time overseas.

It is not automatically the best option. A pet with complex medical needs may need a veterinary boarding facility or a sitter with verified medication experience. Be honest about behaviour, escape risks, separation anxiety and household rules before anyone accepts the booking.

Start planning before your departure

For a stay longer than a week, begin several weeks ahead. This gives you time for a meet-and-greet, a trial visit and a backup plan. Ask the sitter about availability for the whole period, travel plans, overnight expectations and what happens if they become ill.

  • Confirm exact start and end dates, including flight delays and late returns.
  • Decide whether the sitter will stay overnight, visit at set times, or combine both.
  • Arrange a short trial booking so your pet experiences the routine before you leave.
  • Identify a local backup person with a key and permission to help.
  • Agree how often you want photos and updates without requiring constant messaging.
Practical tip: Book a buffer day at each end when possible. A delayed flight or changed ferry connection should not leave a sitter guessing or a pet without care.

Create a care manual that someone can actually use

A long stay should not depend on memory or scattered text messages. Leave one concise document, printed and digital, with the information a sitter needs on a busy day:

  • Meal quantities, times, treats and foods that are off-limits.
  • Walk routes, lead rules, recall limitations and local hazards.
  • Medication name, dose, timing and what to do if a dose is missed.
  • Toilet habits, litter cleaning, cage maintenance and signs of normal behaviour.
  • Comfort routines, sleeping arrangements and boundaries for furniture or rooms.
  • Microchip details, vaccination records and a current photograph.

Write instructions in observable terms. “Give a little food” is ambiguous; “one level cup at 7am and one at 6pm” is useful. Include a sample day and explain what is normal for your pet, such as how long a cat hides after a visitor or how a senior dog climbs stairs.

Health, medication and emergency preparation

Give the sitter your regular veterinary clinic, an after-hours emergency clinic, your preferred contact method and a second local contact. Ask the clinic what authorisation they require when the owner is away. Leave written consent for veterinary assessment and set a sensible spending limit for urgent treatment.

For medication, demonstrate the process and ask the sitter to repeat it back to you. Pre-sort supplies only if your veterinarian says that is appropriate, label everything clearly, and leave enough extra medication for travel delays. Never hide important health information because you fear the sitter will decline; an informed decision protects the animal.

Protect the home as well as the pet

Long-term house and pet sitting involves trust in both directions. Give access only to the areas required. Record meter locations, alarm instructions, rubbish collection day, smoke alarms, irrigation and the location of shut-off valves. Tell a trusted neighbour that the sitter is expected, but avoid posting your absence publicly.

Make escape prevention explicit: check gates after deliveries, keep cats away from open doors, and explain which windows must stay closed. New Zealand weather can change quickly, so include a plan for heat, heavy rain, flooding, storms or a power cut. A sitter should know where the carrier, torches, towels and emergency supplies are kept.

Communication that lasts for weeks

Agree on a rhythm before departure. A daily message with a photo is reassuring for many owners; a more detailed summary every few days may be enough for others. Ask for prompt contact about appetite changes, injury, escape, medication problems or unusual behaviour. Tell the sitter when you will be unreachable on flights or in remote areas.

Keep decisions simple: who can approve treatment, who can collect the pet, and when the backup person steps in. The goal is not surveillance. It is a predictable escalation path when something genuinely needs attention.

How to choose a reliable long-term pet sitter

Look for relevant experience, transparent reviews, clear communication and a willingness to meet your pet before booking. Ask for examples of handling an emergency, administering medication and managing a booking that changed unexpectedly. A professional sitter should ask questions rather than promise that every pet is easy.

Use a written agreement covering dates, services, payment, cancellation, keys, visitors, transport, damage reporting and emergency care. Keep payments and booking details on the platform where possible, and do not choose solely on the lowest price: continuity and reliability matter more over a long stay.

Final checklist before you leave

  • Meet-and-greet and trial care completed.
  • Care sheet, keys, supplies and vet contacts handed over.
  • Emergency authorisation and backup contact confirmed.
  • Food, medication and cleaning supplies stocked with extra allowance.
  • Update schedule, arrival confirmation and return plan agreed.
  • One final calm handover, followed by a message confirming you are safely away.
